## Instructions Format

Each instruction step uses a **bold title** followed by bullet sub-actions:

```markdown
1. **Step Title:**

   * Sub-action or detail.
   * Another sub-action or option.

2. **Next Step:**

   * What to do.
```

Group related sub-actions under one step title rather than making each sub-action its own numbered step. For example, "Sauté the Aromatics" can cover cooking the onion, garlic, and peppers as three bullets under one step.

## Units Reference

| What | Unit | Examples |
|------|------|---------|
| Small measures | tablespoons / teaspoons | `2 tbsp olive oil`, `1 tsp cumin` |
| Solids / bulk | grams | `800 g beef`, `300 g flour`, `2 large onions` |
| Liquids / volume | millilitres | `500 ml beef stock`, `100 ml milk` |

**Rule of thumb:** if it's measured in tablespoons or teaspoons in real life (oils, spices, pastes), keep it that way. Everything else — grams for solids, ml for liquids.

Whole items that aren't weighed in practice (eggs, onions, garlic cloves) are written as counts: `2 large eggs`, `3 garlic cloves`.
